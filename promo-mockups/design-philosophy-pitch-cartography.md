# Pitch Cartography

Pitch Cartography treats play as a field science. A game is not narrated; it is surveyed. Space is divided into a patient grid of equal plates, and inside each plate a single small event is recorded with the detachment of a naturalist cataloguing specimens. Meaning comes from accumulation: sixteen near-identical observations, one of which is allowed to differ. The viewer reads the work the way one reads an atlas, by comparing, finding the variation, and slowly understanding the system.

The ground is a single saturated colour field, deep and matte, as if printed on survey stock. Against it the whole vocabulary is three voices: a bone-white for structure and line, a hot yellow reserved for the one thing that matters, and a darker tone of the ground for quiet tonal cells. Nothing else enters. Line weight is constant and hairline. Every mark is a perfect geometric primitive, a rectangle, a circle, a dot, a dashed trace, repeated until repetition itself becomes texture. The restraint is the luxury.

Composition is governed by a rigid module. Margins are generous and identical on every side; gutters are exact; ruler ticks along the edges imply measurement without announcing a scale. Scale contrast is the only drama: one monumental word set in a condensed grotesque against a field of tiny monospaced labels. Hierarchy is carried by size, position and the single use of yellow, never by decoration. Negative space is not empty; it is the held breath between observations.

Typography is sparse and instrument-like. Labels are small, monospaced, widely tracked, positioned like plate numbers and coordinates. The one large word is treated as architecture, not caption. Nothing overlaps, nothing touches the edge, and every baseline lands on the grid.

The result must read as the product of countless hours: meticulously crafted, every alignment refined past the point anyone would consciously check, the work of someone at the very top of their field. It should reward a long look. A stranger sees a calm, masterful abstract composition; someone who knows the subject feels it immediately.
