Read this part first

Ten of these fourteen references are dark, cold and cinematic. We are not. We are Frontier Yellow, cream, hand-drawn doodles, a scarf-and-crest badge, and “Built by football. Powered by community.” Copy the mood of these sites and we end up with a nice looking generic football site with our crest stuck on it. I would rather not.

So here is the rule I want us to work to: structure from the references, personality from the brand guide. Layouts, stat cards, fixture rows, scroll behaviour, borrow all of that freely. Colour, type, texture and voice, that comes from us.

Only two of them already sit near our energy: Lazarev, bright and warm with objects inside the type, and Sports Website UI, orange on a warm neutral. Treat those as the tonal anchors. The dark editorial sites are for structure and ambition, not palette.

Visual & UI direction

Layout systems and product patterns: fixtures, stats, teams, player profiles, community feed. This is the machinery of the site.

Screenshot reviewed

OFFSIDE, Football Community Landing Page

Royhan · for One Week Wonders · Dribbble

Editorial magazine layout on off-white. Huge black condensed caps. League rows (Bundesliga, Premier League, Serie A, La Liga) as hairline-ruled list items, each with a VIEW MORE. Then a dark card grid for the "Football Area": player stat cards showing 22 goals, 2 assists and 28% conversion on a segmented bar, club standings, carousel arrows. Transfer news uses likelihood bars (Chelsea 85%, Napoli 72%). The footer is one enormous HELLO@OFFSIDE.COM.

TakeThe closest structural match we have. Take the league-row list, the stat-card grid, and percentage bars as a visual device.

Screenshot reviewed

Sports Website UI, "HOOPS"

Bogdan Falin · for QClay · Dribbble

Warm light-grey base with a burnt-orange accent. Giant outlined BALL letterforms filled with a grid of photo tiles, so the type doubles as a photo mask. Black pill nav. Below it a three-step onboarding strip: a black card with a minus/plus counter, an orange card with a radio list for indoor, street or complex, and a white card for location access.

TakeThe type-as-photo-mask trick is cheap and hits hard. The three-step strip is a good model for a "join a match" flow.

Screenshot reviewed

Sports app design concept

Lazarev · UI/UX Design Agency · Dribbble

Light and bright. A massive black condensed headline, CHASE YOUR DREAM WITH ALL YOUR MIGHT, with photos and 3D objects tucked inside and between the letters. A row of saturated program cards along the bottom in orange, purple and lime, each with a black play button.

TakeThe one I keep going back to. Warm, bright and unprecious, which is where we actually live. That card row maps almost straight onto our palette.

02

Tone, product patterns & feel

Warmth, community, and the emotional register. Two facility/community concepts and two app concepts that show how a football product can carry a mood.

Screenshot reviewed

Tenista, Sport Community and Facilities

Sans Design · for Sans Brothers · Dribbble

Warm cream base, olive-green photography, deep-green cards. Rounded stat pills float over the imagery: 18K+ with 12K+ membership, 3.2k impressions, 100% train with the best, #01 highly trusted. The hero is a full-bleed racket shot with GAME ON, PLAY HARD in white caps.

TakeA warm cream base with one strong colour on top, which is the same logic as our cream plus green #305552. The floating social-proof pills would work well on a community platform.

Screenshot reviewed

Louvre Sports Center, landing page

Tosan Garditama · for One Week Wonders · Dribbble

Top-down court photography as the hero with a white italic headline over it. Below that, a facilities explorer of rounded photo tiles, a filter pill row by sport, a testimonial block with a big quote mark, and a blue events and newsletter CTA card.

TakeThe booking and facilities half of the product, already solved. Filter pills plus a card explorer is close to what a venues or fixtures page needs.

Screenshot reviewed

Live Football App

DIGI.CO · Dribbble

Near-black app UI presented on pitch green. The match report hero has a full-bleed photo, a duration pill and a green PLAY button. Match schedule runs as clean list rows, badge then score then badge, separated by hairlines. News cards put a thumbnail left and headline right. A Live grid carries LIVE badges and engagement counts.

TakeThat match-schedule row is the single most reusable thing in the set. Badge, score, badge, hairline underneath. It is more or less our fixtures list finished.

Screenshot reviewed

Sport News Mobile App

DIGI.CO · Dribbble

Same studio as the Live Football App. Deep crimson presentation background, near-black UI, red accent everywhere. Heavy condensed uppercase headlines. Red LIVE badges, red WATCH VIDEO and READ ALL buttons, an EXPLORE MORE thumbnail grid and a RELATED CONTENT list.

TakeThat crimson is nearly identical to our #87292D. Useful proof that our red can hold a whole dark interface together.

03

Live sites, for storytelling

All seven are live and I read them directly. Use them for narrative structure and ambition. Their palettes are not for us, see the note at the top.

United in Football

A cinematic 2026 World Cup tribute. Full-screen scroll chapters, high-contrast photography, big headline type with a lot of air, an interactive winners table, and sections with audio of fans reacting. It uses real numbers (5 billion fans, 211+ nations) as emotional beats rather than as stats, which is a trick worth stealing.

The Immortals

A World Cup archive from 1930 to 2026, designed by Jason Jerez. A dark grid of tournament-year cards, each with a poetic title over an archival image. 1986 is "The Hand and the Crown", 1954 is "The Miracle".

Last Dance for Glory

Gold on near-black, structured as chapters: The Legends, The Contenders, The Underdogs, The Heirs, Trophy Remembers. I would use it mostly as an upper bound. It shows how far you can push editorial drama before a site stops being usable.

BVB Tribute

Dortmund's Klopp years, 2010 to 2013, told season by season with player profiles and fan testimonials running through it. The testimonial format would transfer well to us.

No Football Colors

An independent football culture and design brand. Minimal white and grey, spacious grid, an editorial stories hub, a shop, a newsletter. "No games. Just football through the eyes of design." Proof a football brand can be quiet and still feel real to the culture.

Player Album

An interactive digital World Cup 2026 sticker album. 110 collectible cards, completion tracking, sharing a finished album. If we ever want a collectible hook, this is the model.

Ballerleague UK

The six-a-side league site. Fully dark, card grid for results, league table, stats, teams, players, shop, news. It reads as sports entertainment rather than sports federation, which is closer to our register than most of this list.
