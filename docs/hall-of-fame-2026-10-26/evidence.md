# Hall of Fame: design evidence

Scope: recommendations for the existing Events Hall of Fame only. The evidence researcher read the brief and `app/events/page.tsx` without inspecting a rendered screenshot. The lead later inspected the supplied screenshot and the samples. The code shows a very large section heading, small desaturated team photos, champion and event information in separate desktop columns, and a numbered event list. `resultRows` puts seasons newest-first, then competitions in source order; dates are unknown, so this is not a chronological ranking.

| Claim | Source and evidence | Status | Application here | Tradeoff |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Relevant images of real people can carry information and attract attention; decorative filler is often ignored. | [NN/g: Photos as Web Content](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/photos-as-web-content/): distinguishes relevant, information-carrying photos from decorative images; also warns that large images can get in the way when not requested. | Source page opened and read. | Existing team celebration photos are meaningful content. Give them more area and retain their real identities, credits, and useful crop. | Larger photos increase row height and can crop people or marks if focal position is not checked. |
| Relative size, bright color, and muted color establish hierarchy. | [NN/g: Visual Design Principles](https://media.nngroup.com/media/articles/attachments/Principles_Visual_Design-A4.pdf): scale signals importance; hierarchy guides attention; bright colors can mark important items. | PDF opened and extracted. | The page already gives the heading huge scale and yellow champion text. Rebalance scale so event + winner + photo read as one result, with the heading secondary once the list begins. | Enlarging both heading and result content competes for attention; keep one clear focal point at a time. |
| Motion should be brief and subtle, used to signal interaction or state; irrelevant motion distracts. | [NN/g: The Role of Animation and Motion in UX](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/animation-purpose-ux/): recommends restrained motion for feedback and signifiers; warns against attention hijacking. | Source page opened and read. | Keep the existing hover edge/color/arrow response restrained; if emphasis needs motion, use one short reveal or photo transition, not continuous animation. | Hover is unavailable on touch; the static result must already be clear. |
| Text contrast, motion preference, and pointer target size have explicit accessibility guidance. | [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/): 1.4.3 requires 4.5:1 for normal text and 3:1 for large text; 2.3.3 (AAA) says interaction-triggered motion can be disabled unless essential; 2.5.8 (AA) sets 24×24 CSS px target minimum with exceptions. | W3C Recommendation opened; criteria located in document. Keep Frontier’s existing source order. | A table-like treatment risks recreating the rejected spreadsheet feel. |

## Recommendations

1. Keep the existing source order. Replace the arbitrary two-digit index with the recorded event/season label; do not invent dates or imply a medal, score or rank.
2. Treat each row as one result: event name and date first, champion label and yellow winner name next, with the existing relevant photo enlarged enough to read as a real team celebration. Avoid splitting winner identity so far across the desktop grid that the image feels unrelated.
3. Shrink the Hall of Fame heading relative to the first visible result, or add a compact editorial kicker/section label before the list. Preserve the existing display type, dark canvas, yellow and bone palette.
4. Use color restoration or restrained scale emphasis on the photo at rest instead of relying on hover-only grayscale-to-color. Keep a short hover/focus response for feedback, and honor `prefers-reduced-motion`.
5. At narrow widths, stack photo, event/date, winner, runner-up and Photos action in that reading order; retain readable contrast, descriptive photo alt text/credits, keyboard focus, and at least the current 44px action height.

Evidence limits: no screenshot review, no contrast calculation, and no testing with users. No numerical design ratings or uplift claims.
